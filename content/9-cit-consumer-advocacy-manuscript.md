## Abstract — Provisional Template

**Purpose:** This study aims to explore how consumers describe meaningful shopping incidents and the relationship, if any, between those incidents and communication about shopping services accessed through TikTok in Indonesia.

**Design/methodology/approach:** The planned exploratory study uses an adapted Critical Incident Technique (CIT) and semi-structured interviews with purposively selected adult users. ## 1. Introduction

Social commerce brings shopping into environments where consumers may encounter products through social interaction, creator content, and platform features. Consumers may also intentionally search for products; discovery and purposeful search are not mutually exclusive. In Indonesia, shopping accessed through TikTok involves an ecosystem in which consumers may interact with services, sellers, creators, and logistics providers. `[Add current, verified sources for platform context and terminology.]`

Consumer advocacy can involve positive recommendations or defense of a service. Yet a positive experience need not result in a recommendation, and a consumer may direct support or criticism toward an actor other than the shopping service. An incident-based approach can complement survey research by examining how users narrate specific experiences, actions, and their own interpretations (Flanagan, 1954; Gremler, 2004).

Potential research gap, pending literature verification: existing studies should be reviewed to determine how far they distinguish the source of an experience, the actor held responsible, and the target of subsequent consumer communication. `[Replace this provisional gap with a precise, cited comparison of the relevant studies.]`

The project will explore this distinction and the conditions under which positive experiences are not followed by advocacy.

**Research question 1:** What meaningful critical incidents do users describe in shopping through TikTok in Indonesia, and to whom do they attribute responsibility?

**Research question 2:** How do users explain the relationship, if any, between these incidents and advocacy, non-advocacy, or negative communication toward the shopping service?

**Expected contribution:** The study seeks to develop a contextual account of how consumers distinguish actors and targets of communication. Its actual contribution must be stated only after analysis and comparison with the literature.

## 2. Literature Review and Theoretical Orientation

### 2.1 Social Commerce and the Shopping-Service Context

Review definitions of social commerce, interactions with creators and sellers, and the Indonesian shopping-service context. Distinguish platform features from the consumer's own understanding of the service. Avoid claiming that all conventional e-commerce is search-based or that all social commerce is discovery-based. `[Insert verified sources and publication details.]`

Working framing (to be replaced or anchored by cited sources): Social commerce is a distinct evolution from traditional electronic commerce by embedding transactional capabilities within social media environments. Rather than utilizing deliberate, intent-based search mechanisms, social commerce platform architectures encourage discovery-based consumption, frequently conceptualized as "shoppertainment." The shopping service integrated into the TikTok application represents a key manifestation of this model, merging immersive algorithmic video loops and real-time live broadcasting with instantaneous checkout functionalities.

### 2.2 Advocacy, Non-Advocacy, and Incentives

For this proposed study, **actual advocacy** refers to a participant-reported positive recommendation or defense directed toward the shopping service without an identified incentive. Advocacy intention will be recorded separately from actual conduct. **Non-advocacy** refers to the absence of recommendation or defense in the specified context or period, whether the experience is positive, neutral, or negative. **Negative communication** is a comparator, not positive advocacy. Incentivized communication and unknown incentive status will be recorded separately; motivation cannot be inferred solely from an incentive. `[Support the working definitions with relevant literature.]`

Working framing (provisional): In relationship marketing, brand advocacy is defined as an advanced behavioral outcome wherein consumers voluntarily recommend, praise, or defend a brand to their social networks without financial remuneration. Non-advocacy is conceptualized as the absence of positive recommendation or defense, occurring regardless of whether the underlying consumer experience was highly satisfactory, neutral, or dissatisfactory.

### 2.3 Attribution of Responsibility as an Open Lens

Attribution of responsibility will serve as an initial interpretive lens to explore which actors participants regard as responsible and why. The analysis will remain open to multiple actors, uncertainty, and cases where no actor is blamed or praised. Trust, fairness, reciprocity, and customer delight may be considered if relevant to the data and literature, not imposed as mandatory findings. `[Identify and cite the precise attribution theory source and specify any dimensions used.]`

Working framing (provisional): In a social commerce environment, a single transaction involves multiple independent actors: the core infrastructure platform (TikTok/Tokopedia), independent digital merchants, content creators or affiliates, and third-party logistics providers. When a critical incident occurs — either a severe system breakdown or an extraordinary service highlight — consumers engage in cognitive assessments to allocate locus of causality, stability, and controllability. How a consumer attributes responsibility determines whether the subsequent communication outcome becomes organic platform advocacy, targeted communication against a merchant, or total silence.

## 3. Methodology — Planned, Not Completed

### 3.1 Design, Context, and Unit of Analysis

The study will use an exploratory qualitative design adapting the Critical Incident Technique (Flanagan, 1954; Gremler, 2004). The principal unit of analysis will be a specific incident, not the participant. One participant may describe multiple incidents. A critical incident will be a specific experience for which context, actors, sequence, and outcome can be identified and which the participant considers meaningful to their views, decisions, or responses concerning the service.

The focal object will be the shopping service accessed through TikTok in Indonesia as understood by the participant. Source of experience, actor regarded as responsible, and target of communication will be recorded separately. Multiple or unclear actors and targets will remain possible.

### 3.2 Sampling and Eligibility

Participants will be purposively recruited to seek relevant variation in positive, negative, and mixed experiences, as well as advocacy, non-advocacy, and negative communication. Planned eligibility: age at least 18; residence in Indonesia; at least one transaction through the service in the preceding 12 months; and willingness to describe a meaningful specific experience. Whether the incident itself must also fall within 12 months will be decided before recruitment and documented. `[Insert finalized timeframe and justification.]`

Recruitment may use `[approved channels]`, subject to ethics review and availability. A provisional planning range of 20–30 interviews may be adjusted; it is not a CIT standard or a guarantee of saturation. Data adequacy will be evaluated using depth, incident diversity, comparison cases, and the substantive contribution of new data. `[Record recruitment steps, changes, and the stopping rationale.]`

### 3.3 Data Collection and Incident Selection

Semi-structured interviews are planned in `[language(s)]` via `[online/in-person mode]`, with recording only after informed consent. A provisional duration of 45–60 minutes is a planning estimate, not an achieved average. A pilot will examine clarity, neutrality, and ability to elicit concrete incidents; eligibility of pilot data for the final dataset will be determined transparently. `[Insert approved procedure.]`

Prompts will ask what happened, when, which actors were involved, what each did, the current outcome, why the incident mattered, responsibility as perceived by the participant, and whether any recommendation, criticism, silence, or other response followed. Interviewers will ask whether the participant perceives any connection, without presuming causality. Unresolved incidents and cases with no opportunity to communicate will remain eligible where other criteria are met.

Candidate incidents will be logged and assessed for relevance, specificity, sufficient context/action/outcome, and participant-perceived significance. General opinions without an identifiable event, insufficiently detailed accounts after feasible clarification, out-of-scope accounts, and confirmed duplicates may be excluded with reasons. Lack of an actual communication response is not itself grounds for exclusion. Participant counts and incident counts will be reported separately.

### 3.4 Analysis and Quality Procedures

The proposed analysis uses codebook-based qualitative content analysis. The initial codebook will be informed by research questions, relevant concepts, and exploratory reading of incidents. Following a pilot and revision, two coders may independently classify the same prespecified subset using a documented version of the codebook. A single incident may receive multiple codes for response, target, timing, or incentive status. Where appropriate, agreement will be reported before reconciliation. Cohen's kappa may be calculated for clearly defined mutually exclusive categories or separately as present/absent for suitable individual codes. The study will report the subset selection, number of units, category distribution, observed agreement, kappa where computable and interpretable, and limitations of sparse categories. Kappa is not a measure of overall qualitative validity. Deviant cases and incidents without advocacy will be examined, with decisions documented in an audit trail. `[Specify actual subset selection and procedures before testing.]`

Data collection will begin only after required institutional ethics approval and informed consent. Participants may stop an interview without penalty. Working direction (provisional, to be confirmed or revised by data): the transition from a critical incident to an outcome of brand advocacy may be mediated by the consumer's cognitive attribution of responsibility.
